# Starchorus

**Instruments for seeing the networks around you.**

Most of what happens on a network is invisible, and the tools that describe it tend to arrive as
tables: accurate, exhaustive and very nearly unreadable. Starchorus draws it instead. The radio and
the wires around you are laid out so that their shape can be taken in at a glance, and every mark
on the screen still answers to a measurement.

## The instruments

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **[Pager](https://github.com/starchorus/pager)** | The Wi-Fi around a Hak5 WiFi Pineapple Pager, drawn as a night sky: access points as stars, their clients as satellites. Strictly passive: it listens and never transmits. | GPL-3.0-or-later |
| **Mac** | The machines on your local network, drawn in the same manner, as a native macOS application. | In preparation |

## How the work is done

- **Passive by design.** What can be learnt by listening is learnt by listening.
- **Exact rather than impressive.** A figure on the screen can always be traced to its source.
- **Quiet.** No telemetry, no accounts, no calls home.
- **Judged on the work.** Contributions stand or fall on their merit; see each repository's
  `CONTRIBUTING.md`.

## On screen

Starchorus is also at home in front of a camera. Its demonstration mode shows a convincing sky made
of simulated data and placeholder names, and so discloses nothing about anyone: well suited to
on-set playback and screen graphics in film and television.
